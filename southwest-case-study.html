Designing Vacation Bidding for 11,000 Pilots

Replacing a 20-year-old scheduling tool with a decision-support system built around how pilots actually think — and the union contract rules that govern their time off.

Company Southwest Airlines (via Amdocs Studios)
Timeline 2022–Present
Role Principal Designer
Focus Decision-Support Design, Enterprise UX, Research, Design Systems

The Problem

Southwest pilots bid for vacation every year. Who gets what depends on seniority — senior pilots pick first, everyone else works around them. It's a high-stakes process. Miss your bid window or misunderstand the rules and you might lose Christmas with your family.

The tool pilots use to bid is nearly 20 years old. It wasn't designed around their union contract (SWAPA) requirements, and it doesn't work well on their primary work device — an iPad Pro used in landscape mode, often in the cockpit. The result: many pilots pay out of pocket for third-party apps rather than use the free internal tool.

That's the problem I was brought in to solve.

The constraint environment

Every design decision was shaped by four hard constraints:

  • Union contract rules (SWAPA): Bid windows, seniority order, and bid group structures are all contractually defined. The design has to reflect the contract accurately or pilots make costly mistakes.
  • Device context: iPad Pro, landscape mode, dark mode. Pilots use this device for other work activities — sometimes in the cockpit. The interface has to work in that environment.
  • High personal stakes: Vacation weeks aren't just time off. They represent family commitments, personal milestones, and life priorities planned months in advance.
  • Seniority complexity: What's a realistic bid for one pilot may be completely unattainable for another. The tool needs to account for where each pilot sits in the seniority list.

5 products, 10 apps, 4 user types — pilots, flight attendants, planners, and schedulers — all governed by union contract rules and federal scheduling requirements.

The Design Challenge

The old system let pilots rank weeks of the year in order of preference. Simple, but fundamentally misaligned with how vacation bidding actually works under the SWAPA contract.

The new system needed to support bid groups — clusters of 2–3 weeks that pilots submit as a unit, depending on the bid round. Instead of saying "I want week 52, week 26, week 1," pilots can now say:

  • Primary: Christmas week + July 4th week
  • Fallback: Christmas week + any week in July
  • Backup: Any week in December + any week in summer

That's a strategy builder, not a ranked list. It's a fundamentally different cognitive task — and a much harder design problem.

Research

Before designing, I needed to understand how pilots think about vacation — not how the contract defines it.

I partnered with a researcher to run a RITE (Rapid Iterative Testing and Evaluation) study with pilots, testing four different concept approaches to the bidding flow. While the researcher facilitated sessions, I synthesized findings and created the readout presentation for stakeholders.

What we tested

Four concepts, each taking a different approach to the core interaction:

  • A wizard-based flow that guided pilots step by step
  • An overview-first approach showing all options upfront
  • A calendar-based visual model
  • A hybrid approach combining ranking with group selection

Four concepts tested in RITE sessions with pilots. No single approach won — each revealed something different about how pilots think about vacation strategy.

Ease of use ratings across all four concepts (1–7 scale). Concept B scored highest with the tightest range — strong approval but limited complexity. Concept A showed the widest range, suggesting pilots had fundamentally different mental models of the bid group flow. These findings directly shaped the synthesis direction.

Recommended direction: synthesize the strongest elements from each concept and retest. Iteration over assumption.

What pilots told us

"It's not a term we use."

— Pilot, on the term "bid group"

"Are we putting the cart before the horse for this one? Rank all weeks, then rank all holiday weeks?"

— Pilot, on concept sequencing

"I think for most people, holidays are important, but personal things are more important."

— Pilot, on what vacation bidding is really about

Key Findings

No single concept emerged as a clear winner — each had distinct strengths. More importantly, the research revealed a fundamental terminology problem: pilots don't use the same language as the contract. A tool built around contract terms creates confusion before pilots even start bidding.

Design direction: Synthesize the strongest elements from each concept, reframe the language around how pilots actually talk about vacation, and run another round of testing.

The Solution Direction

The redesign is built around two core features working together.

Bid group builder

Instead of ranking individual weeks, pilots build bid groups — primary, fallback, and backup combinations — that reflect their actual vacation strategy. The interface is designed for landscape iPad Pro in dark mode, matching how pilots use their devices in the field.

Confidence indicator

Each bid group shows a confidence level — High, Medium, or Low — based on the pilot's seniority position. This gives pilots realistic signal about whether their primary bid is attainable, so they can make smart fallback decisions rather than guessing.

Together, these features shift the tool from a passive ranking interface to an active decision-support system. Pilots aren't just submitting preferences — they're building a strategy they can trust.

Step 2 of 3: Selecting preferred weeks for Week B. The split layout — calendar on the left, selected weeks on the right — was designed for landscape iPad Pro, matching how pilots use their devices in the field. The persistent context bar at the top (Seniority 342/1240, Remaining Accrual, Bidding Closes) stays visible throughout the entire wizard. The "Odds: HIGH" indicator updates in real time as pilots make selections, giving immediate signal about whether their Week B choices are realistic.

Step 3 of 3: Naming the bid group and reviewing week combinations. The Week Selections view (shown here, with Bid Choices accessible via toggle) addresses a key RITE study finding: pilots needed to see their individual week choices and the resulting combinations separately. The "Apply Contingency" option allows pilots to set fallback rules if their preferred combination isn't awarded. Pilots can also save the bid group for the next vacation cycle, reducing the effort of rebuilding the same strategy year over year.

Bid Choices view: all possible week combinations with awarding odds. Every possible combination of the pilot's selected weeks is ranked and scored with Awarding Odds (Low, Medium, High) based on available slots and seniority position. Rather than submitting blind, pilots can see at a glance which combinations are within reach and adjust their strategy accordingly. The overall "Odds: Medium" badge gives a single at-a-glance read on the bid group as a whole.

Current Status

The redesign is in progress. Updated designs are slated to undergo another round of user testing with pilots before moving to engineering handoff.
