Building H-E-B's Accessibility Program from the Ground Up

Creating systems and culture that made digital grocery shopping accessible to millions of Texans.

Company H-E-B Digital
Timeline 2019–2022
Role Senior Product Designer → Accessibility Lead
Focus Accessibility, Systems Design, Change Management

The Challenge

H-E-B's website and mobile apps were inaccessible to customers using assistive technology. During COVID-19, when customers with disabilities couldn't safely shop in stores, our digital products — which should have been their lifeline — were excluding them.

The problem was organizational, not just technical:

  • No accessibility standards or testing processes
  • Limited awareness of why it mattered
  • Screen reader users couldn't complete checkout
  • Color contrast failed throughout the app

We were leaving customers behind.

My Approach

I saw an opportunity to build something from nothing. Rather than trying to fix everything at once, I focused on proving value, building grassroots support, and creating infrastructure that could scale.

Proved the value through research

Piloted user research with Fable's platform, connecting our team with real assistive technology users. Watching a screen reader user struggle through checkout was more powerful than any compliance argument. Leadership started paying attention.

Built grassroots support

Started weekly accessibility office hours where designers and developers could ask questions and learn together. Partnered with a passionate developer to co-lead sessions. About 3–5 people attended regularly, creating a safe space to build capability across the team.

Educated the organization

Presented "why accessibility matters" sessions across H-E-B. I didn't lead with compliance — I led with people. When teams understood their work affected whether customers could feed their families, they started caring deeply about quality. I also ran design workshops on focus order and keyboard navigation.

Established strategic direction

Worked with the Design System PM and Lead Engineer to research vendors and create a formal accessibility roadmap. Before I left, I presented recommendations to leadership, who were ready to invest in accessibility as a strategic priority.

What I Built

Design System & Standards

Updated color contrast across the app to meet WCAG AA. Created accessible component patterns. Built an accessibility checklist in Figma for design handoff.

Critical Fixes

Fixed checkout so screen reader users could complete purchases. Resolved keyboard navigation issues platform-wide. Addressed hundreds of accessibility violations.

Community & Education

Weekly office hours became the go-to resource. Workshops on focus order and screen reader UX. Presentations connecting accessibility to H-E-B values.

Impact

WCAG 2.1 AA Compliance achieved
100s Violations resolved
3–5 Weekly office hours attendees

Screen reader users could finally complete checkout independently. Color contrast met WCAG 2.1 AA standards across the design system. Hundreds of accessibility violations resolved across web and mobile.

Cultural shift: Designers proactively presented accessible options; change spread organically. Cross-functional collaboration strengthened through office hours.

Lasting foundation: Created infrastructure and processes that could scale beyond any one person. Left leadership ready to invest in accessibility as a strategic priority, not just a compliance checkbox.

What I Learned

You don't need permission to lead. I saw a gap and started building. Sometimes leadership is about making what needs to exist.

Change spreads through people, not policy. Office hours and education did more than mandates ever could. When people understood the human impact, accessibility became part of our culture.

The biggest challenge was not knowing what I didn't know. But being honest about gaps and building community around shared learning mattered more than pretending to be an expert. I learned as much from teaching as my team learned from me.

This is the work I'm most proud of — not because it was technically complex, but because it genuinely helped people and created something lasting. I hope H-E-B designers are still designing accessible experiences and talking to customers who use assistive technology to shop.

Building something from nothing and watching a team embrace inclusive design because they genuinely cared — that's the impact I want to keep making.
